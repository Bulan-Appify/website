Position

We will talk you out of the wrong AI project.

There is enormous pressure on executives right now to have an AI story. That pressure produces pilots that demo beautifully, never reach production, and quietly disappear from the roadmap eighteen months later.

The projects that work share a boring profile: a high-volume, rule-heavy, document-shaped process where a human is currently doing something repetitive and slightly error-prone, and where being right 95% of the time with a human checking the rest is genuinely better than the status quo.

Our assessment ranks your candidate processes on exactly that basis and tells you which ones to leave alone. That conversation costs R38 000 and routinely saves several times that.

Selection criteria

How we decide whether a process is worth automating.

Five questions. A candidate needs a convincing answer to at least four of them before we will quote for a build.

01

Is the volume real?

Forty instances a month is a spreadsheet problem, not an AI problem. We want processes measured in hundreds or thousands, where small per-item savings compound into something visible.

02

Is a good-enough answer actually useful?

If the process demands 100% accuracy with no human review and no tolerance for error, rules and validation beat models. Be honest about this early — it is the single most common reason pilots die.

03

Can we measure whether it worked?

Handling time, error rate, backlog, cost per transaction. If there is no baseline today, we establish one before building, or you will never be able to prove the thing paid for itself.

04

What happens when it is wrong?

Every automation needs a defined failure mode: a confidence threshold, a human queue, an audit trail and someone accountable. Where decisions materially affect a person, POPIA has something to say about it — see section 71.

05

Will anyone actually use it?

The most common cause of death for internal AI tools is not accuracy, it is adoption. If the people doing the work were not consulted, the tool loses to the existing spreadsheet every time.

Responsible by default

Data boundaries, stated plainly.

Before a single document moves, we document what data leaves your environment, which provider receives it, under what contractual terms, in which jurisdiction, and for how long it is retained. You sign that off. It becomes part of your POPIA record of processing.

No training on your data

We use enterprise API tiers with contractual no-training commitments and zero or minimal retention — and we show you the terms rather than asking you to take our word.

Local option available

Where data genuinely cannot leave the country or the building, we design around self-hosted open-weight models. Slower and more expensive to run, but sometimes the only lawful answer.

Grounded, with citations

Assistants answer from your documents and link to the source paragraph, so a person can verify in seconds instead of trusting a confident paragraph.

Human in the loop

Anything that affects a person's money, employment, credit or care keeps a human decision-maker. That is both good practice and, under POPIA section 71, frequently a legal requirement.

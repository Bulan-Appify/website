Why it matters

A defect costs roughly thirty times more in production than in design.

That multiplier is the most reliable finding in four decades of software engineering research, and it is why quality is a commercial decision rather than a technical preference. A requirements error caught in a workshop costs an afternoon. The same error caught by a customer costs a hotfix, a regression cycle, a support queue, an incident report and, increasingly often, a regulatory conversation.

Most South African organisations we speak to do not have a testing problem. They have a timing problem: testing happens at the end, under time pressure, by the same people who built the thing.

Method

How a test engagement runs.

01

Risk workshop

We work out with you what would actually hurt — the journeys that lose money, break trust or attract a regulator. Coverage follows risk, because testing everything equally is the same as testing nothing deliberately.

02

Strategy & environment

Test levels, entry and exit criteria, the data we need and how we will get it without copying live personal information into a test database. That last point is a POPIA obligation people routinely miss.

03

Execution

Scripted and exploratory testing in parallel, defects raised live into your tracker with reproduction steps, evidence and severity. You see the picture daily, not in a report at the end.

04

Automation

Once a journey is stable, it becomes an automated regression case in your pipeline. The suite belongs to you, runs on every commit, and is documented well enough for your team to extend.

05

Report & decision

A go/no-go recommendation with the residual risk stated plainly. We will say "do not release this" when that is the answer, and we will explain what would have to change for the answer to be different.
